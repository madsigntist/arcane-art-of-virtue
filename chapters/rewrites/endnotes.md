## Chapter 6: Respond Over React

The breathing technique referenced in this chapter draws from _The Miracle of Mindfulness_ by Thich Nhat Hanh. Hanh's work on present-moment awareness, breath, and the practice of returning attention to what is immediate shaped the thinking on focus and composure across several chapters, not only the technique itself.

---

## Chapter 8: Roles Not Titles

The four-part consequence evaluation framework (warranted, reasonable state, associated with the behavior, proportional) is adapted from the WRAP framework in _Punishment-Free Parenting_ by Jon Fogel. The WHOLE framework for responding to children's behavior is also drawn from Fogel's work.

The following works directly shaped the thinking in this book. Where specific frameworks or ideas were adapted, that is noted in the chapter endnotes above. In other cases, these books informed the general orientation without any single concept being lifted whole.

**Stoic Sources**

The chapters on composure, self-regulation, and what is within our control draw on Stoic thought without citing any single text. Readers interested in the primary sources are directed to:

- Marcus Aurelius. _Meditations._
- Epictetus. _Enchiridion._
- Seneca. _Letters from a Stoic._

**A Note on Cognitive Biases and Logical Fallacies**

The reference chapters on cognitive biases and logical fallacies were compiled from general knowledge, published research, and widely available sources. No single text is the origin. Readers who want to go deeper will find ample material under both subjects.
