# Chapter 7 — The Ability to Do

Power is not status. It's not authority or influence over others. It's simpler than that. Power is the ability to do or act. That which enacts change. By that definition, it's the most practical thing I can work toward, because without it, everything I understand about how to live stays theoretical.

Understanding something and doing something are two different skills. I've known people with clear values and sharp self-awareness who still couldn't move. And I've been that person. Knowing what I should do didn't make it happen. Ego doesn't live in that gap. It feeds on it.

Ego shows up on both ends. The inflated version overreaches. It chases outcomes it hasn't earned, acts from a place of entitlement, and treats any resistance as an obstacle rather than information. The bruised version retreats. It rehearses past failures, anticipates rejection, and uses self-pity as an excuse to stay still. Both stall progress. Both are rooted in the same thing: fear running through the filter of self.

The correction isn't confidence. Confidence is an outcome, not a starting point. The correction is action taken before the fear resolves. Boldness. Enter action. Don't wait to feel ready. Feeling ready is not the same as being prepared. Plan thoroughly. Think it through. Know your course. Then act without hesitation. A finished plan doesn't need more time. It needs a decision. Everything after that is fear stalling for cover.

## Change What You See First

Before behavior changes, perception has to. I don't see the reality as it is. I see it as I am. The lens I'm looking through is built from everything I've experienced, every assumption I've accumulated, every fear I haven't examined. If I want to act differently, I have to look differently first.

A paradigm shift is a change in how I see a situation, not just a change in what I do about it. It comes before strategy. Most of the problems I've tried to solve at the behavioral level kept coming back because the underlying perception was unchanged. I treated the symptom. The source was still running.

This is why two people can face the same circumstances and respond completely differently. The situation isn't what determines behavior. The interpretation of the situation is.

## Where to Put the Energy

Not everything that matters is mine to move. Not everything I can affect is worth moving. Where these two overlap is where the work goes.

What lives on the internal side: my judgments, my intentions, my decisions, how I respond. What lives on the external side: outcomes, other people's choices, the past, events already in motion. The distinction sounds obvious. Living by it is not.

Fear floods the external side. It masquerades as effort. Worrying about what someone else will decide feels like engagement. Rehearsing outcomes I can't control feels like preparation. It isn't. That energy doesn't accumulate. It burns and leaves nothing. Keeping attention on what's actually mine to affect reduces the noise, sharpens the next action, and builds the kind of resilience that doesn't require things to go well.

What I can affect, tended consistently, tends to grow. That growth is slow and unimpressive until it isn't. But it only happens if I stop pouring effort into ground that doesn't belong to me.

## Reverse-engineer the Dream

Define the outcome first, then work backward. The goal may be distant, even unrealistic-sounding. That's fine. The question is: what has to be true for that to happen? What task gets me there? What has to happen before that task is possible? I keep pulling the thread until I land on something I can do today. That step is where the work starts. 'X' takes A, A takes B, B takes C, and I can do C right now.

This happens on paper or a task list. Something written down, not held loosely in my head. The reverse-engineering is the planning, and the plan is what makes action deliberate and purposeful. The destination is the first decision. Everything else is subtraction until the work is small enough to touch.

## Process, Not Outcomes

An outcome is the sum of incalculable influences from innumerable unknown sources. My efforts shape its direction but cannot determine it. So the outcome is not where I focus. The process is. I control the quality of the choices, the consistency of the effort, the honesty of the execution. The result follows from them over time, but it cannot be forced.

When the result arrives, success and failure answer the same question: what did I actually do, and how does that compare to what I intended? A good outcome following poor process is unearned. A poor outcome following sound process is a lesson. The result changes the story. The question stays the same.

That framing changes what challenges are. They are not obstacles between me and the outcome. They are part of the process, the mechanism through which capability builds. Avoiding difficulty keeps me where I am. The range of what I can do only extends by moving through what resists me. All growth is on the other side of resistance.

## When the Approach Fails

Banging my head against a brick wall won't create a door. Not every obstacle is meant to be pushed through. An obstacle is a teacher. The ground is wrong, the timing is off, the approach has failed enough times to constitute data. Knowing when to stop is not the same as quitting. Stopping, reassessing, and changing direction is an action. Sometimes you have to surrender in order to advance.

What I lack tells me what I need. I cannot build strength before I first admit I am weak. A weakness examined honestly is a map. The distance between where I am and where I need to be is not evidence that I can't get there. It's the path to my goal. Liabilities become assets the moment they stop being avoided and start being worked.

You are not in your final form. Growing into who you want to be is painful and uncomfortable. Progress has no finish line. The work doesn't stop. Stay hungry, or you will starve.
